# Module 1 Assessment — What is AI and why does it matter?

**Type:** Scenario-based written assessment
**Time:** 20–30 minutes
**Format:** Individual

---

## Assessment scenario

> Grace works as an administrator at a small hospital in her city. The hospital has just introduced an AI system that flags patient records for follow-up based on test results. Grace's manager tells her the system is "99% accurate" and that she should trust its recommendations without checking manually — they are too busy to double-check every flag.
>
> Grace is not sure this is the right approach. She noticed that the system flagged a patient she was certain had already been discharged, and it did not flag another patient whose results looked concerning to her.

---

## Assessment questions

**Question 1** (understanding)

In plain language, explain what the AI system at Grace's hospital is likely doing. What kind of data might it have been trained on? What kind of patterns might it be recognising?

*Suggested length: 3–5 sentences*

---

**Question 2** (application)

Grace's manager says the system is "99% accurate." Why might this not be enough information to decide whether to trust the system without manual checks? Give at least two reasons.

*Suggested length: 4–6 sentences*

---

**Question 3** (critical thinking)

If you were advising Grace, what would you suggest she do? Consider: her responsibility to patients, her relationship with her manager, and the limitations of the AI system.

*Suggested length: One short paragraph*

---

## Marking rubric

### Question 1 — Understanding AI systems

| Level | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| Strong | Accurately describes pattern recognition / prediction from data. Mentions training data. Avoids overclaiming (does not say AI "understands" or "thinks"). |
| Adequate | Describes what the system does in practical terms. May not mention training data but shows basic understanding of AI outputs. |
| Developing | Describes the system in vague terms ("it uses technology to check things"). Little evidence of understanding how AI works. |

### Question 2 — Limitations and critical evaluation

| Level | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| Strong | Identifies at least two distinct limitations: e.g. accuracy varies across patient groups; 1% error rate has real consequences at scale; accuracy on training data may not match current patients; "accurate" does not mean "safe to act on without checking." |
| Adequate | Identifies one clear limitation with explanation. |
| Developing | States that AI can be wrong but does not explain why 99% accuracy is still a concern in this context. |

### Question 3 — Applying judgement

| Level | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| Strong | Recommends a course of action that balances patient safety, professional responsibility and practical constraints. Shows understanding that human oversight matters when AI decisions affect health outcomes. |
| Adequate | Makes a reasonable recommendation. May be too cautious or too trusting of the AI but shows some critical thinking. |
| Developing | Defers entirely to the manager or entirely rejects the AI without reasoning. |

---

## Facilitator note

This assessment is designed to reveal how learners apply their understanding of AI limitations to a real-world context. There is no single correct answer to Question 3. Look for evidence of reasoning, not a specific conclusion.
